## 1. Executive summary

### Key management takeaways

- For the fiscal year under review, higher completed-construction revenue and greater real estate transaction volume lifted net sales 8.1% to ¥1,273.1 billion; an improved gross profit margin on completed construction contracts raised operating profit 16.6% to ¥98.7 billion and ordinary profit 12.8% to ¥94.1 billion, while profit attributable to owners of the parent rose 59.2% to ¥54.8 billion, resulting in higher revenue and profit. <sup>[FY2026 p.4/s0011]</sup>
- Consolidated net assets increased ¥31.4 billion from the prior year-end to ¥563.5 billion, mainly because retained earnings increased following the recognition of profit attributable to owners of the parent. <sup>[FY2026 p.6/s0029]</sup>
- Net cash provided by operating activities was ¥157.4 billion, an increase of ¥153.5 billion from the ¥3.9 billion inflow in the prior year. <sup>[FY2026 p.6/s0031]</sup>
- In the first year of the HASEKO Evolution Plan, developers and customers continued to value the Company's land-information gathering, product planning, construction quality, adherence to schedules, and efficient production system; improved order-entry profitability and a higher gross profit margin on completed construction contracts contributed to consolidated ordinary profit of ¥94.1 billion. <sup>[FY2026 p.8/s0051]</sup>
- The shareholder-return policy targets a total payout ratio of approximately 50% of aggregate profit attributable to owners of the parent over the six-year plan, maintains stable and progressive dividends, and permits flexible share repurchases when appropriate. <sup>[FY2026 p.7/s0043]</sup>

### Major business drivers

- Against this backdrop, in the first year of the HASEKO Evolution Plan, consolidated ordinary profit reached ¥94.1 billion, exceeding the ¥90.0 billion forecast, due to an improvement in the gross profit margin on completed construction contracts. <sup>[FY2026 p.4/s0010]</sup>
- Increased deliveries of newly built condominiums, greater sales of income-producing real estate, and more brokerage transactions lifted segment net sales 16.0% to ¥293.2 billion and operating profit 9.2% to ¥35.6 billion. <sup>[FY2026 p.5/s0020]</sup>
- The operating margin was 7.8%, up 0.6 percentage points, and the ordinary profit margin was 7.4%, up 0.3 percentage points. <sup>[FY2026 p.4/s0012]</sup>

### Outlook

- For fiscal 2026, new supply is expected to exceed the prior-year level in the Tokyo metropolitan area because redevelopment and large-scale projects are scheduled for launch, while the Kinki region is expected to remain roughly flat. <sup>[FY2026 p.8/s0048]</sup>

### Risks and uncertainty

- Although the construction industry is supported by stable demand, it faces many challenges, including rising material and labor costs, compliance with overtime limits and work-style reform, shortages and succession issues among skilled construction workers and equipment contractors, decarbonization, and growing expectations from society and investors. <sup>[FY2026 p.8/s0052]</sup>
- In fiscal 2026, the Company will need to monitor increasingly uncertain economic conditions and monetary-policy developments more closely than before. <sup>[FY2026 p.8/s0050]</sup>
- Looking ahead, improvements in employment and income conditions and the effects of various policies are expected to support a moderate recovery; however, developments in the Middle East, fluctuations in financial and capital markets, and U.S. trade policy require close monitoring. <sup>[FY2026 p.4/s0002]</sup>

### Latest filing in context

FY2026 was a margin-led earnings beat, not merely a volume story. Higher completed-construction revenue and real-estate transaction volume lifted revenue 8.1% to ¥1,273.1 billion; a better completed-construction gross margin raised operating profit 16.6% to ¥98.7 billion and ordinary profit 12.8% to ¥94.1 billion, above the ¥90.0 billion forecast. Profit attributable to owners rose 59.2% to ¥54.8 billion. The leading operating signal was the recovery in order-entry profitability while developers continued to value HASEKO's land intelligence, planning, quality, schedule control, and production system.

Operating cash flow rebounded to ¥157.4 billion. Capital returns are now a firmer part of the plan: roughly 50% total payout over six years, progressive dividends, flexible buybacks, and a ¥100 next-year dividend forecast.

Management expects Tokyo-area condominium supply to rise on redevelopment and large launches while Kinki supply stays broadly flat. The key question is whether recovered order economics can withstand material and labor inflation, workforce constraints, tighter labor rules, and a broader macro risk set spanning monetary policy, the Middle East, markets, and U.S. trade policy.

## 2. Trend analysis

### The decade in perspective

Across a decade of filings, HASEKO repeated one operating thesis: early land intelligence, product planning, construction quality, schedule control, and efficient production win developer work. The return on that franchise was cyclical, however—order economics and gross margin improved in 2017, weakened in 2021 and 2024 as input costs rose, then recovered in 2026. Capital allocation moved from stable dividends after retaining funds for growth to explicit payout targets, progressive dividends, and buybacks. Risk language accumulated rather than rotated: buyer affordability and labor constraints were joined by overtime regulation, decarbonization, succession, geopolitics, and investor expectations. The latest filing is best read as a margin recovery plus a harder capital-return commitment, not a new business model.

<sup>[FY2017 p.5/s0009; FY2021 p.5/s0013; FY2024 p.5/s0012; FY2026 p.8/s0051; FY2017 p.8/s0051; FY2026 p.7/s0043; FY2017 p.4/s0002; FY2026 p.8/s0052; FY2026 p.4/s0002]</sup>

### What remained consistent

**The capability set endured; its monetization did not.** Land intelligence, product planning, construction quality, schedule control, and efficient production recur almost verbatim from 2017 through 2026. The important distinction is that these capabilities remained valued even when order-entry profitability and gross margin deteriorated in 2021 and 2024. The durable asset is the developer-facing execution system, not uninterrupted margin or pricing power. <sup>[FY2017 p.5/s0009; FY2021 p.5/s0013; FY2024 p.5/s0012; FY2026 p.8/s0051]</sup>

**Management kept two demand lenses separate.** Commentary consistently distinguished project and construction demand from end-buyer absorption. Buyer caution persisted in 2017; 2019 improvement depended on scarce central-city projects and adjusted ticket sizes; 2024 sales were supported by low variable mortgage rates despite higher prices; and 2026 showed regional variation despite wage support. Stable construction demand has never meant frictionless condominium sell-through. <sup>[FY2017 p.9/s0056; FY2019 p.8/s0049; FY2024 p.8/s0052; FY2026 p.8/s0049]</sup>

### What changed

**The margin story completed a full cycle.** Order-entry economics and completed-construction gross margin were improving in 2017. By 2021, weaker order economics and higher input costs were explicitly depressing margin, and the same pressure was still visible in 2024. In 2026 both order-entry profitability and gross margin improved, helping ordinary profit beat guidance. The most useful leading indicator is order quality, not revenue growth. <sup>[FY2017 p.5/s0009; FY2021 p.5/s0013; FY2024 p.5/s0012; FY2026 p.8/s0051]</sup>

**Capital returns moved up a policy ladder.** In 2017 the policy prioritized retained funds for financial strength and growth while maintaining stable dividends. By 2021 management had introduced an approximately 40% total-payout target; the later NS years paired faster growth investment with expanded returns. The 2026 framework raises the target to roughly 50% over six years and adds progressive dividends and flexible buybacks. Returns are now embedded in plan architecture. <sup>[FY2017 p.8/s0051; FY2021 p.8/s0047; FY2024 p.7/s0043; FY2026 p.7/s0043]</sup>

**Plans broadened the uses of a stable core franchise.** NBj emphasized active growth investment while maintaining profit and strengthening the future earnings base. NS then framed core-business competitiveness, larger real-estate investment, and future growth under a long-term vision; its final-year review still credited the same construction strengths and rising construction work in progress. HASEKO Evolution begins with recovered construction economics. This is widening capital deployment around a stable core, not repeated strategic reinvention. <sup>[FY2017 p.9/s0063; FY2020 p.7/s0058; FY2025 p.8/s0054; FY2026 p.8/s0051]</sup>

**Risk disclosure accumulated new layers.** The 2017 discussion stressed overseas uncertainty and rates. By 2019, materials, labor scarcity, and work-style reform were explicit industry constraints; decarbonization joined them by 2022. In 2026 the list added contractor succession and societal and investor expectations. The older cost and labor issues did not fade—the risk surface became structurally wider. <sup>[FY2017 p.4/s0002; FY2019 p.8/s0045; FY2022 p.8/s0047; FY2026 p.8/s0052]</sup>
