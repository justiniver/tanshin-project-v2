## 1. Executive summary

### Key management takeaways

- Group revenue and operating profit increased as the other segments more than offset lower revenue and profit in Energy. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0008]</sup>
- The Group therefore revised its first-half and full-year fiscal 2026 forecasts to reflect developments since the February 13, 2026 results announcement, principally higher orders in Environmental Solutions, Energy, and Precision Machinery and the sale of an equity-method joint venture stake. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.9/s0046]</sup>
- Under these conditions, the Group formulated the three-year E-Plan2028, beginning in 2026, with sustainable value creation through overall optimization as its theme, and is advancing measures toward its management targets by strengthening the management base through group-wide optimization and raising global competitiveness and profitability. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0004]</sup>

### Major business drivers

- First-quarter Precision Machinery orders exceeded the prior-year level as AI-related demand drove higher customer factory utilization and expanded investment in production capacity. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0005]</sup>
- Environmental Solutions orders exceeded the prior-year level because the segment secured a large project. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0006]</sup>

### Outlook

- Nevertheless, the Group expects its businesses to remain firm, supported by long-term growth in semiconductor demand and stable social-infrastructure demand. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.9/s0045]</sup>
- Customer factory utilization is trending upward, and the overall market is expected to grow, led by AI-related demand, with expanded investment forecast in logic and foundry as well as memory. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.11/s0050]</sup>
- The overseas water-infrastructure market is expected to continue growing gradually, supported by water demand from population growth in Asia and work on aging facilities in North America. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.11/s0066]</sup>

### Risks and uncertainty

- The business environment is expected to remain uncertain because of concerns including U.S. tariff and other policy developments, tighter semiconductor export controls arising from U.S.-China tensions, the effect of prolonged conflict in the Middle East and Ukraine on resource prices, and currency fluctuations. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.9/s0044]</sup>
- However, uncertainty remains over how higher materials and transportation costs caused by the Middle East situation will affect market conditions and the Group's businesses. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.11/s0051]</sup>
- In the Middle East, where uncertainty persists, projects may be delayed or plans changed. <sup>[FY2026/12 Q1 p.11/s0069]</sup>

### Latest filing in context

EBARA entered E-Plan2028 with a broad-based first quarter, but the quality of growth varied by segment. Precision Machinery orders rose as AI demand lifted customer utilization and expansion investment; Environmental Solutions benefited from a large order. Group revenue and operating profit increased because the other segments more than offset a decline in Energy. That portfolio offset is important, but it should not obscure the different order and margin cycles underneath it.

Management revised its interim and full-year forecasts, citing higher orders in Environmental Solutions, Energy, and Precision Machinery as well as the sale of an equity-method joint venture stake. The operating outlook rests on long-run semiconductor growth and stable social-infrastructure demand; customer fab utilization and AI-related investment are the most useful near-term Precision Machinery signals.

The risk map is unusually concrete. U.S. tariff policy and semiconductor export controls can affect demand, while Middle East conflict can raise materials and freight costs. E-Plan2028's shift from customer-facing reorganization to group-wide optimization therefore has to show up in segment margins, order-to-revenue conversion, and cash discipline—not only higher orders.

## 2. Trend analysis

### The decade in perspective

Across ten years, EBARA repeatedly used a portfolio lens: weakness in one business was often explained alongside offsets elsewhere, while orders, backlog conversion, and lifecycle service linked demand to earnings. What changed was the center of gravity. Precision Machinery moved through several visible semiconductor cycles and now carries an AI-led growth narrative; operating discipline progressed from fixed-cost and product-profitability work to price realization, backlog conversion, market-based organization, and group-wide optimization. Capital allocation also became more binding, moving from payout and dividend formulas to a three-year free-cash-flow commitment. Risk language widened from project and market cycles to supply chains, export controls, tariffs, currencies, and geopolitics. FY2017/12 was a nine-month transition, not a normal annual comparison.

<sup>[FY2016 p.4/s0018; FY2017/12 p.10/s0039; FY2018/12 p.10/s0070; FY2019/12 p.5/s0006; FY2020/12 p.5/s0010; FY2021/12 p.5/s0012; FY2023/12 p.5/s0004; FY2023/12 p.5/s0009; FY2024/12 p.5/s0007; FY2025/12 p.13/s0084; FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0004; FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0008]</sup>

### What remained consistent

**Portfolio offsets were the recurring earnings grammar.** Management repeatedly explained the group through offsetting segment cycles rather than a single demand curve. Precision Machinery lifted profit in 2016; in 2019, gains in Fluid Machinery and Environmental Plants outweighed its decline; in 2023, other businesses covered weaker Precision Machinery orders; and in the latest quarter they covered Energy's decline. Diversification is visible in the reported bridge, even though it does not eliminate segment volatility. <sup>[FY2016 p.4/s0021; FY2019/12 p.5/s0006; FY2023/12 p.5/s0007; FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0008]</sup>

**Lifecycle service remained the recurring-revenue layer.** E-Plan2016 explicitly aimed to become a service business across the full product and plant lifecycle. That logic survived later plan changes: Service & Support stayed firm during 2020 disruption, price and service-demand capture supported 2022 growth, and domestic service demand lifted Building Service & Industrial orders in 2024. The recurring layer is not separate from equipment; it grows from the installed base and customer operating activity. <sup>[FY2016 p.4/s0018; FY2020/12 p.8/s0044; FY2022/12 p.5/s0009; FY2024/12 p.5/s0008]</sup>

### What changed

**Precision Machinery became more central, but not less cyclical.** The semiconductor narrative turned repeatedly: a high investment base slowed in 2018, demand and pre-orders surged with 5G, AI, and data centers in 2021, manufacturer capex and utilization weakened in 2023, and AI-led utilization and expansion investment recovered in 2024-26. Management now tracks fab utilization and investment restart more explicitly. Greater strategic weight has increased, not removed, exposure to the cycle. <sup>[FY2018/12 p.10/s0070; FY2021/12 p.5/s0009; FY2023/12 p.5/s0006; FY2024/12 p.5/s0007]</sup>

**The operating agenda moved from growth to integrated discipline.** E-Plan2016 framed a turn from foundation-building to growth. By 2020 management emphasized product profitability and fixed-cost control; 2023 added price revisions and backlog conversion while E-Plan2025 reorganized around customer markets. E-Plan2028 now elevates group-wide optimization. The test is whether cross-business coordination improves margins and capital use without blurring accountability for individual segment cycles. <sup>[FY2016 p.4/s0018; FY2020/12 p.5/s0010; FY2023/12 p.5/s0004; FY2026/12 Q1 p.4/s0004]</sup>

**Capital returns shifted from formulas to a cash architecture.** The policy moved from a 25% dividend-payout reference to at least 30% total payout in 2017, then to at least 35% dividend payout plus a 2% dividend-on-equity floor in 2019. E-Plan2028 retains the 35% payout target but adds continuing buybacks and total shareholder returns of at least 100% of three-year free cash flow after necessary investment. That is a material increase in capital-allocation constraint. <sup>[FY2017 p.7/s0032; FY2019/12 p.11/s0076; FY2025/12 p.13/s0081; FY2025/12 p.13/s0084]</sup>

**Risk moved from market cycles to multiple operating channels.** Earlier commentary emphasized oil-project delays and semiconductor investment cycles. By 2021, raw-material and logistics inflation and component shortages required price pass-through, cost action, and supply-chain management. Recent filings add export controls, tariffs, currencies, and war-related resource and logistics effects. The important change is transmission: one geopolitical event can now affect demand, input cost, service delivery, and project timing across different businesses. <sup>[FY2016 p.4/s0023; FY2021/12 p.5/s0012; FY2026/12 Q1 p.9/s0044; FY2026/12 Q1 p.11/s0069]</sup>

### What the latest commentary now implies

**Read orders together with conversion, mix, and cash.** The next filings should be read as a chain, not around one headline order number: customer utilization and investment restart drive Precision Machinery orders; backlog conversion and project timing drive revenue; price, service mix, and segment offsets drive margin; working capital and investment determine whether earnings become free cash flow. That final conversion now matters more because E-Plan2028 links shareholder returns directly to three-year cash generation. <sup>[FY2023/12 p.5/s0009; FY2025/12 p.5/s0008; FY2025/12 p.13/s0084; FY2026/12 Q1 p.9/s0046]</sup>
